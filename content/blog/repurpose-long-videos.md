If you already record long content, such as a podcast, a livestream, a lecture, a sermon, an interview or a webinar, you are sitting on material for dozens of short videos. The challenge is finding the strongest moments and turning them into clips that make sense on their own. This guide describes a repeatable workflow.

## Why clips work

A long video asks for commitment. A short clip lets a new viewer sample your work in under a minute. Done well, clips become the front door to the long content. They are also efficient: you do not have to invent new material, only choose and present it well.

A caution before you start: only clip content you own or have permission to use. Cutting up someone else's video without permission can breach copyright and platform rules, even if you add captions or commentary.

## Step 1: Get a transcript

The most useful thing you can have is a timestamped transcript. Reading is faster than scrubbing through an hour of video. Automatic transcription gives you a first draft. Skim it and fix obvious errors, especially names.

## Step 2: Look for self-contained moments

Not every good moment makes a good clip. A clip has to make sense with no context. When you scan a transcript, look for:

- **A strong opening line.** A clear claim, a question or a surprising statement within the first few seconds.
- **A complete thought.** The segment includes the setup and the point, not just the punchline.
- **An emotional peak.** Laughter, tension, passion, a revealing admission.
- **A payoff.** The clip ends on a conclusion, a joke landing or a clear takeaway rather than trailing off.
- **Clarity without the rest of the video.** Avoid clips that rely on "like I said earlier".

Clue AI's clip scoring uses these same ideas: hook strength, emotional peak, standalone clarity, payoff and pacing. It returns a ranked list with a short reason for each pick. That is useful for finding candidates quickly, but you are the editor. Skip suggestions that do not fit your audience.

## Step 3: Choose the length

There is no perfect length. Many creators aim for roughly fifteen to sixty seconds. A tighter clip is usually better than a longer one, provided the thought is complete. If a point needs ninety seconds to land, do not cut it short just to hit a number.

## Step 4: Cut at sentence boundaries

Clips that start or stop mid-word feel sloppy. Snap your cuts to the beginning and end of sentences. Trim silence at the start so the first word begins almost immediately. If a speaker uses filler words at the opening, such as "so, um, yeah", start after them.

## Step 5: Reframe to vertical

Horizontal video has to be reframed for a 9:16 screen. There are a few approaches:

- **Crop to the speaker.** Follow the face so the speaker stays centred. With two speakers, you may need to switch focus.
- **Blurred background.** Place the full horizontal frame in the middle with a blurred copy behind it. This keeps everything visible but makes the main picture smaller.
- **Split view.** Stack two speakers, or show the speaker above and supporting footage below.

Face tracking should be smooth. Constant jumping is distracting. Clue AI tracks faces and smooths the crop window, and falls back to a blurred background or centre crop when no face is found. Always preview the result, because tracking can be fooled by faces in the background or by quick movement.

## Step 6: Add captions

Captions are especially important for clips, because people discover them in a feed with the sound off. Use word-highlighted captions in a large, high-contrast style and correct the transcript first. Our caption guide goes into sizes and placement.

## Step 7: Fix the audio

Long recordings often vary in volume. Normalise loudness so clips sound consistent with other content on the platform, and listen for background noise or echo. If the source audio is poor, no amount of styling will rescue it, so improve your recording setup for next time.

## Step 8: Write the title and hook

The title or on-screen text at the start should tell viewers why to watch. Use a plain description of the point of the clip. Avoid promising more than the clip delivers. If your clip has a hook line suggested by the tool, rewrite it in your own voice.

## Step 9: Link back, but do not rely on it

Mention where the full video lives in the description and, if it feels natural, in a pinned comment. Keep the clip complete on its own. Viewers are more likely to follow you if the clip itself was worth their time.

## Step 10: Publish on a schedule and learn

Spread clips over days rather than posting a dozen in one afternoon, and note which ones perform. After a few weeks, look for patterns. Maybe clips with questions in the first line do well. Maybe your audience prefers short clips. Use that to choose better moments from the next long recording.

## A practical routine

1. Record the long video as usual.
2. Upload it, transcribe it, and generate ranked clip suggestions.
3. Choose the best six to ten and adjust the start and end.
4. Reframe, caption and export.
5. Schedule them across the following two weeks.
6. Review results and adjust your selection criteria.

## Things to watch for

- **Context that matters.** A clip taken out of context can mislead. Make sure a clip represents what the speaker meant.
- **Consent.** If guests appear, make sure they agreed to the use of their words and image.
- **Sensitive content.** Be careful with private information and medical, legal or financial advice.
- **Over-clipping.** Posting weak clips just to fill a schedule can hurt more than it helps.

Repurposing is about selection and presentation rather than volume. A handful of well-chosen clips will do more for your channel than dozens of random cuts. Start with one recording, follow the steps, and refine the process as you learn what your audience watches.
